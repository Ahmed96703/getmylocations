## What does this speed test measure?

This **internet speed test** runs four checks, one after another, and shows each result live:

-   **Download speed** (Mbps): how fast data reaches you. It decides how quickly pages load and whether video streams in HD or 4K.
-   **Upload speed** (Mbps): how fast you send data. It matters for video calls, cloud backups and live streaming.
-   **Ping** (ms): the round trip to the server. Lower is better for gaming and calls.
-   **Jitter** (ms): how much ping varies. Steady, low jitter keeps calls smooth.

Several parallel streams fill the connection the way a real download does, so the figure reflects your line rather than a single slow request. It works as a **download speed test**, **upload speed test**, **ping test** and **jitter test** in one, and as a **broadband speed test**, a **bandwidth test** and a quick **internet speed checker**.

## WiFi speed test: is it the WiFi or the line?

A **WiFi speed test** measures the whole path: your device, the wireless signal, the router and the line from your provider. To find where speed is lost:

-   **Test next to the router,** then in the room where things feel slow. A big drop points to the WiFi signal, not the line.
-   **Test on a cable** if your laptop has an Ethernet port. That result is the true speed of your connection.
-   **Try the 5 GHz band** (or 6 GHz on newer routers). It is much faster than 2.4 GHz at short range.
-   **Restart the router** and test again. It clears a surprising number of slowdowns.

## DSL, cable, fiber and mobile speed tests

The same **net speed test** works on any kind of connection and any provider. What counts as a normal result depends on the technology:

| Connection | Typical download | Typical upload | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| DSL / ADSL / VDSL | 5–100 Mbps | 1–20 Mbps | Slower the farther you are from the exchange |
| Cable | 100–1,000 Mbps | 10–50 Mbps | Evenings can be slower on busy networks |
| Fiber (FTTH) | 300–2,000+ Mbps | Often equal to download | Lowest ping and jitter |
| Mobile 4G | 10–60 Mbps | 5–20 Mbps | Varies with signal and crowds |
| Mobile 5G | 100–1,000 Mbps | 10–100 Mbps | Best near a mid-band or mmWave mast |
| Satellite | 25–250 Mbps | 5–30 Mbps | High ping on older geostationary services |

A **DSL speed test** often shows a much lower upload than download: that is how DSL is built, not a fault. A **fiber speed test** often shows upload almost as fast as download, while a **cable speed test** usually shows a much lower upload. For a **mobile speed test** or **5G speed test**, switch off WiFi first so the phone uses the mobile network.

## Getting an accurate result

-   **Pause other downloads** and streaming on the same connection.
-   **Test close to the router,** or on a cable, to separate Wi-Fi problems from the line itself.
-   **Test at different times:** evenings are often slower when the whole neighborhood is online.
-   **Turn off a VPN,** which adds distance and overhead, unless you want to measure the VPN.
-   **Close other browser tabs,** especially video and cloud sync, before you press Start.

Speeds are in megabits per second; file sizes are in megabytes, eight times larger. A 100 Mbps connection downloads about 12.5 MB each second. Convert any figure with the [bytes and bits converter](https://getmylocations.com/bytes-bits-converter).

## How does it compare with the Ookla speed test?

Many people know the **Ookla speed test** (Speedtest.net). It and this test measure the same four numbers, but they use different server networks, so results can differ by a few percent:

|  | This speed test | Ookla Speedtest |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Servers | Cloudflare’s nearest data center | Choice of thousands of hosted servers |
| Measures | Download, upload, ping, jitter | Download, upload, ping, jitter |
| Needs an app | No, runs in the browser | Browser or app |
| Signup | None | None for basic tests |
| Extra info | Your IP, network and test location | Provider and server name |

If one **speed test** shows a slow result, run another to confirm before contacting your provider. Two low results from different networks point to your connection, not the test.

## Speed test slow? What to try next

-   **Compare with your plan.** Getting 80–90% of the advertised speed on a cable is normal.
-   **Check for many devices** sharing the connection: phones updating apps, TVs streaming, cloud backups.
-   **Update the router firmware,** or replace a router older than about five years.
-   **Record a few results** at different times. A screenshot of repeated slow tests is the best evidence for your provider.
-   **High ping but good speed?** Check whether the server you use is far away; see where an address is with the [IP WHOIS lookup](https://getmylocations.com/ip-whois-lookup).

## Frequently asked questions

What is a good internet speed?

For one person, 25 Mbps download handles HD streaming and video calls. A household streaming 4K on several screens is comfortable with 100 Mbps or more. For video calls and gaming, ping under 50 ms and jitter under 10 ms matter more than raw speed.

How do I run a WiFi speed test?

Connect your phone or laptop to the WiFi network, stay in the room you want to check and press Start. Run it again next to the router: if that result is much faster, the line is fine and the WiFi signal is what slows you down.

Is this the same as the Ookla speed test?

No. Speedtest by Ookla is a separate service with its own servers. This net speed test measures the same things (download, upload, ping and jitter) against Cloudflare’s network, so results are usually close but not identical. Testing on both is a good way to confirm a slow result.

Does it work as a DSL, cable, fiber or 5G speed test?

Yes. The test measures whatever connection your device is using, so it works as a DSL speed test, a cable or fiber broadband speed test, or a mobile 4G and 5G speed test, on any provider.

Why is my result lower than my plan?

Plans are sold as “up to” speeds. Wi-Fi distance and walls, other devices using the connection, an old router or a busy time of day all lower the result. For the most accurate figure, test on a cable connection with other devices idle.

What are ping and jitter?

Ping is how long a tiny message takes to reach the server and come back. Jitter is how much that time varies. High jitter causes choppy calls and lag in games even on a fast connection.

Which server does the test use?

Cloudflare’s speed-test endpoints, answered by whichever of its data centers is closest to you, so the result reflects your connection rather than a distant server.

How much data does the test use?

On a fast connection up to about 250 MB, less on slower ones. On a limited mobile plan, run it only when you need to.
