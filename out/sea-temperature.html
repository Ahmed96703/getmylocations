Sea temperature today: water temperature at any beach

Find the sea temperature today at any beach, resort or stretch of coast in the world, measured by satellites and updated daily. See whether the water is warm enough to swim, in °C or °F, and how it has warmed or cooled over the last 30 days. Free, no signup.

Sea temperature at a beach or coast

Click anywhere on the sea to check it.

Sea surface temperature from the NASA JPL MUR analysis (a daily blend of satellite and buoy measurements on a 1 km grid), served by NOAA CoastWatch. It is the temperature just below the surface, free of daytime warming, so shallow bays can feel a degree or two warmer on a sunny afternoon. Data usually lags by a day.

Staying safe in cold water

  • Get in slowly and let your breathing settle before you swim; the gasp reflex passes in about a minute.
  • Stay close to shore and swim parallel to it, ideally at a lifeguarded beach.
  • Get out before you shiver, and warm up gradually with dry, warm layers and a hot drink.
  • If you get into trouble, float on your back until your breathing is under control, then call for help or swim to safety.

Temperature is only part of the picture: check the tide times, the wind and the flags at the beach, and never swim alone in cold water.

Frequently asked questions

What is the sea temperature today?

Search a beach, resort or coastal town, or click the sea on the map. The tool shows the latest daily sea surface temperature at the nearest open water, what it feels like to swim in, and a 30-day trend.

What sea temperature is warm enough to swim?

Most people find 18 °C (64 °F) and above comfortable for a proper swim, and 22 °C (72 °F) and above warm. Below 15 °C (59 °F) the water is cold and a wetsuit helps; below 10 °C (50 °F) cold water shock is a real risk.

When is the sea warmest?

The sea lags behind the air by one to two months. Around Britain and northern Europe it is warmest in late August and September and coldest in February and March; in the Mediterranean it peaks in August at around 25 to 28 °C.

Why is the beach colder than the reading?

Wind blowing offshore can pull up colder water from below (upwelling), and river mouths bring in cold fresh water. In summer the top metre in a sheltered bay can also be warmer than the reading. The figure is a good guide to the open water just off the coast.

Does it work for lakes?

No. The satellite analysis covers seas and oceans; lakes and rivers are not included.
