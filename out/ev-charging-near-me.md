# EV charging points near me: rapid, fast and free chargers on a map

Find the **electric car charging points within 5 km** of you or any place, nearest first, with the connectors (**CCS, Type 2, CHAdeMO, Tesla**), the highest **power in kW**, the network and whether charging is free. Filter for **rapid DC chargers** and get driving directions in one tap. Works across the UK, Europe, North America and Australia. Free, no signup.

## EV charging points near a place

Press the button to use your location, search for a place, or click the map.

Tap a dot to see its number in the list; the three nearest are larger. Click the map to search somewhere else.

Places and opening hours from [OpenStreetMap](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright) contributors, refreshed weekly. Distances are straight-line. Your position is rounded to about 2 km before the lookup is sent, and distances are worked out in your browser.

## How to find a charging point

-   **Press “Chargers near me”** and allow location, or search a town, postcode, motorway services or address.
-   **Filter** for rapid DC chargers, your connector (CCS or Type 2), free charging or open now, or type a network such as Tesla or Ionity.
-   **Check the badges**: connectors and how many, the highest power, number of bays and how you pay.
-   **Press Directions** to drive there with Google Maps, and check the network’s app for live availability.

## Charging speeds explained

| Type | Power | Typical connector | Adds about (average car) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Slow (AC) | 3 to 7 kW | Type 2, J1772, home wallbox | 15 to 40 km of range per hour |
| Fast (AC) | 11 to 22 kW | Type 2 | 50 to 120 km per hour, if the car accepts it |
| Rapid (DC) | 50 to 100 kW | CCS, CHAdeMO | 10 to 80% in about 40 to 60 minutes |
| Ultra-rapid (DC) | 150 kW and above | CCS, Tesla / NACS | 10 to 80% in about 20 to 30 minutes |

The car sets the limit as well as the charger: plugging into a 350 kW charger does not help a car that accepts 50 kW. Charging also slows down above about 80%, so on a long trip two short stops are often quicker than one long one.

## Where the data comes from

The charging points come from OpenStreetMap, the free map built by volunteers, read through the QLever search engine of the University of Freiburg. Connector types, power and network are shown as volunteers recorded them, so a newer charger can have less detail. Our server keeps each area’s answer for up to a week.

## Frequently asked questions

How do I find a rapid charger near me?

Press "Chargers near me" and tick "Rapid / DC fast only". That keeps charging points with a CCS, CHAdeMO or Tesla connector, or a mapped power of 50 kW or more. Each result shows the connectors, the highest power mapped and the network.

Which connector does my car use?

Almost every new electric car in Europe, the UK and Australia charges with Type 2 on AC chargers and CCS (also called CCS2 or Combo) on rapid chargers. Older Nissan Leafs and some Mitsubishis use CHAdeMO. In North America, older cars use J1772 and CCS1, and many newer ones use the Tesla connector, now standardised as NACS.

Are the chargers free?

Most are not. Results marked "Free to charge" are recorded as free on OpenStreetMap, often at supermarkets, hotels or council car parks. Tick that box to show only those. Parking charges can still apply.

Does it show whether a charger is available right now?

No. Live availability comes from each network's own app. This map shows where charging points are and what they offer, from OpenStreetMap, refreshed weekly. Check the network app before a long detour.

Why is a charger I know missing?

It may not be on OpenStreetMap yet, as new chargers open every week. The "Details or fix" link opens the map so anyone can add or correct one.
