## What is EXIF data?

EXIF is a block of information that cameras and phones write inside every photo: the date and time, the camera and lens, settings such as exposure and focal length, and, when location services are allowed, the latitude, longitude and altitude where it was taken. Many phones also save the compass direction. The time is the camera’s own clock and carries no time zone, which is why the tool shows it exactly as recorded.

The same data that makes this tool work can reveal where you live or where your children go to school. Before posting photos publicly from an app that keeps metadata, or sending originals by email, check the location with this tool, and remove it if needed. ## Frequently asked questions

How do I find where a photo was taken?

Drop the photo, or tap to choose it, and the tool reads the GPS coordinates your phone or camera saved inside the file. It shows the spot on a map with the address, the date and time it was taken, the camera, and often the direction the camera was facing.

Why does my photo have no location?

Either location was turned off for the camera when the photo was taken, or the location was removed later. WhatsApp, Facebook, Instagram, X, Messenger and most other social and messaging apps strip GPS data when a photo is sent or posted, and screenshots never have it. Use the original file from the phone or camera, or from a cloud backup such as iCloud Photos or Google Photos.

Are my photos uploaded anywhere?

No. The file is read inside your browser and never leaves your device. Only when the tool looks up an address does it send the photo's coordinates, not the photo, to OpenStreetMap's geocoder.

Can it find the location of a photo with no GPS data?

No. Without saved coordinates the location can only be guessed from what is in the picture, such as signs, landmarks or the landscape, which is what a reverse image search does. This tool only reports what the file itself records, so its answers are never guesses.

How do I remove the location from my photos before sharing?

On iPhone, tap Options at the top of the share sheet and turn off Location. On Android, Google Photos lets you remove the estimated or GPS location in a photo's details, and many cameras have a setting to stop saving it. On Windows, right-click the file, choose Properties, Details, then "Remove Properties and Personal Information". On a Mac, Preview's Inspector can remove the location.
