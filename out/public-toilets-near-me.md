# Public toilets near me: the nearest toilet, free or paid

One tap shows the **public toilets within 2 km** of you, nearest first, with the walking time and whether each one is **free**, **wheelchair accessible** or has **baby changing**. Works anywhere, with the best coverage in European cities. Free, no signup.

## Public toilets near a place

Press the button to use your location, search for a place, or click the map.

Tap a dot to see its number in the list; the three nearest are larger. Click the map to search somewhere else.

Places and opening hours from [OpenStreetMap](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright) contributors, refreshed weekly. Distances are straight-line; walking times assume streets about 30% longer. Your position is rounded to about 1 km before the lookup is sent, and distances are worked out in your browser.

## How to find the nearest public toilet

1.  **Press “Toilets near me”** and allow location access, or type a street, station or square. You can also click the map.
2.  **Narrow it down** with the boxes: free only, wheelchair accessible, or baby changing.
3.  **Read the labels and hours.** Each result says how far it is and how long the walk takes, plus the opening hours where they are mapped.
4.  **Press Directions** to open walking directions in Google Maps.

## Free public toilets in Europe: where to look

Free street toilets vary a lot from city to city. Paris has hundreds of free self-cleaning _sanisettes_ on its pavements, while toilets at railway stations in Germany, Switzerland and much of Italy usually charge a small fee. When the map is empty nearby, these places almost always have one:

-   **Large stations and airports,** often paid in mainland Europe; in the UK, toilets at the big stations Network Rail runs are free.
-   **Shopping centres, department stores and big supermarkets,** usually free.
-   **Libraries, museums and town halls** during opening hours.
-   **Cafés and fast-food restaurants,** many of which expect you to buy something; some print a code on the receipt.

Some German towns run the _Nette Toilette_ scheme, in which cafés and shops let anyone use their toilet for free; look for the red-and-white smiley sticker by the door.

## Accessible toilets and baby changing

Tick **Wheelchair accessible** to see only toilets recorded as step-free with room for a wheelchair, and **Baby changing** for those with a changing table. In the UK many accessible toilets are locked to keep them clean and available, and open with a RADAR key from the National Key Scheme, sold by Disability Rights UK and many councils. For people who need a hoist and an adult changing bench, Changing Places toilets are listed on the Changing Places website.

## Where the data comes from

The toilets come from [OpenStreetMap](https://www.openstreetmap.org/), the free map built by volunteers, read through the QLever search engine of the University of Freiburg, with the Overpass API as a backup. Toilets that are private, or marked as closed to the public, are left out. Our server keeps each area’s answer for up to a week. If a toilet is missing or has closed, the “Details or fix” link opens it on OpenStreetMap, where anyone can update it for the next person.

## Frequently asked questions

Are the toilets on the map free?

Those marked "Free" are recorded as free on OpenStreetMap and those marked "Paid" as charging, with the price where a volunteer noted it. Many have no label either way. Tick "Free only" to hide everything not marked free, which also hides the unlabelled ones.

Does it show toilets in cafés and shops?

Only where they are mapped as toilets open to the public, or marked "Customers only". Ordinary café and restaurant toilets are not usually mapped. Toilets that are private, or marked as not open to the public, are left out.

How do I find a wheelchair-accessible toilet?

Tick "Wheelchair accessible" to show only toilets recorded as fully accessible. "Limited wheelchair access" usually means a step or a narrow door. In the UK many accessible toilets are locked with a RADAR key from the National Key Scheme; Changing Places toilets, with a hoist and adult-sized changing bench, are listed by the Changing Places consortium.

Why is a toilet I know missing, or closed?

It may not be on OpenStreetMap yet, or it closed after it was mapped. Our copy is up to a week old. The "Details or fix" link under each result opens it on openstreetmap.org, where anyone can add a toilet or mark one closed.

Is my location stored?

No. Your browser rounds your position to about 1 km before it asks our server for the toilets in that area, and the distance to each one is worked out on your device. Nothing is saved by this site.
