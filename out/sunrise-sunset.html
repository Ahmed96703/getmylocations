Twilight and golden hour explained

MomentSun positionWhat it means
Golden hour6° above to the horizonLow, warm, soft light that photographers prefer
Sunrise / sunsetTop edge on the horizonThe moments given in weather forecasts
Civil twilight0° to 6° belowStill light enough to be outdoors without lamps; "first light" and "last light"
Nautical twilight6° to 12° belowThe horizon fades; sailors could still take star sightings
Astronomical twilight12° to 18° belowSky almost dark; faint stars appear
NightMore than 18° belowFully dark sky for stargazing

The blue hour that photographers talk about is roughly the civil twilight just before sunrise and after sunset, when the sky turns deep blue.

Why day length changes through the year

The Earth’s axis is tilted by about 23.4 degrees, so for half the year each hemisphere leans toward the sun and gets longer days. Days change fastest around the equinoxes in March and September, by two to four minutes a day in mid latitudes such as London or New York, and slowest around the solstices in June and December. Near the equator the day stays close to 12 hours all year. North of the Arctic Circle the sun does not set at all around midsummer and does not rise around midwinter; the tool shows both.

Frequently asked questions

What time is sunset today?

Press "Sunrise & sunset here" to use your location, or search for any place. The tool shows today's sunset in that place's own time zone, with the time of last light, when it gets fully dark enough to need street lights, about 25 to 40 minutes later in Europe and North America.

When is golden hour?

Golden hour is the time when the sun is low, less than 6 degrees above the horizon, and the light is soft and warm. In the morning it runs from sunrise until the time shown as "Morning golden hour ends"; in the evening from "Evening golden hour starts" until sunset. Near the equator it lasts about 25 minutes; at high latitudes in summer it can last several hours.

What is the difference between sunset and dusk?

Sunset is when the top of the sun drops below the horizon. Dusk (civil dusk, or last light) comes later, when the sun is 6 degrees below the horizon and it is too dark to read outdoors. Nautical and astronomical dusk follow at 12 and 18 degrees, when the sky is fully dark.

How accurate are the times?

Usually within a minute of national almanacs, for sea level and a flat horizon. Mountains, hills or tall buildings to the east or west can hide the sun for several minutes longer, and the exact moment varies a little with air temperature and pressure.

Why is the earliest sunset not on the shortest day?

Because the Earth's orbit is an ellipse and its axis is tilted, solar noon drifts by a few minutes over the year. In the Northern Hemisphere the earliest sunset comes in early to mid December and the latest sunrise in early January, either side of the shortest day around 21 December.
