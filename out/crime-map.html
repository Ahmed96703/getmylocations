UK crime map: crime in my area by postcode

See every crime reported within one mile of a UK postcode, street or your current location, by type and by street, for any of the last 12 months. The data comes from police.uk, published by every force in England, Wales and Northern Ireland. Free, no signup.

Crimes within 1 mile of a place

Click the map to check another spot. Bigger dots mean more crimes at that point.

Data from data.police.uk, published by the Home Office under the Open Government Licence. Forces publish each month about two months later. Your position is rounded to about 100 m before it is sent.

How to check crime in your area

  • Press “Crime near me” to use your location, or type a postcode, street or town. You can also click the map.
  • Pick a month to see how it compares with earlier ones. The newest month is about two months ago.
  • Read the breakdown. The list shows each type of crime with its share of the total; click one to show only that type on the map.
  • Check the streets list to see which points have the most reports, then use Street View to see what is there, often a station, a shop or a pub.

What the crime types mean

  • Anti-social behaviour: nuisance, rowdy or inconsiderate behaviour, such as noise, littering or harassment, that is reported but is not always a crime.
  • Violence and sexual offences: assaults from common assault upwards, harassment, and sexual offences.
  • Shoplifting and other theft: theft from shops, and thefts that fit no other group, such as from a garden or workplace.
  • Burglary: entering a home or business to steal. Vehicle crime is theft of or from a vehicle, or tampering with one.
  • Criminal damage and arson, public order, drugs, robbery, bicycle theft, theft from the person (such as pickpocketing) and possession of weapons follow their everyday meaning.

Is this area safe? Reading the numbers fairly

A crime count measures where crime is reported, not how risky a place is for you. A street with a railway station or a large supermarket can show dozens of crimes a month while the houses behind it show none, because shoplifting and incidents in busy public places make up much of the total. To judge an area, compare two places of the same kind, look at several months rather than one, and look at the types: burglary and vehicle crime matter more to a resident than shoplifting in a town centre.

Police force websites and the neighbourhood pages on police.uk also show your local policing team and their current priorities.

Report a crime

In an emergency, or if a crime is happening now, call 999. To report a crime that is not an emergency, call 101 or use your force’s online form. To report information anonymously, contact the independent charity Crimestoppers on 0800 555 111.

Frequently asked questions

Where does the crime data come from?

From data.police.uk, the official site where every police force in England, Wales and Northern Ireland publishes the crimes reported to it each month. It is the same data behind the police.uk neighbourhood pages, released under the Open Government Licence.

Why are crimes shown on the same few points?

To protect victims, police.uk never publishes exact addresses. Each crime is moved to the nearest of a fixed set of anonymous map points, usually the middle of a street or a landmark such as a car park or a shopping area, labelled "On or near ...". A busy point can stand for crimes from several nearby streets.

Why is the latest month two months ago?

Forces send their data to the Home Office once a month and it is published about two months after the month ends, so in November you will usually see September. Some forces publish late, so the newest month can be incomplete for a few weeks.

Does it cover Scotland?

No. Police Scotland does not publish street-level crime to police.uk, so every place in Scotland shows no crimes. Police Scotland publishes its own statistics by council area and ward.

Why does my area show no crimes at all?

Either the place is in Scotland, or the police force that covers it has not sent its data to police.uk for that month. The tool names the force in that case. Greater Manchester Police, for example, has not published street-level crime to police.uk for recent months, so Manchester shows none; the force website is the place to look for its figures.

Is a high number a sign that an area is dangerous?

Not on its own. Town centres, stations and shopping streets always show more crime because far more people pass through them, and shoplifting or anti-social behaviour make up a large share. Compare like with like: residential streets with residential streets, and look at the types of crime as well as the total.
