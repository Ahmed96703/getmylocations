## UTC, GMT and time zone names

-   **UTC** (Coordinated Universal Time) is the world’s reference clock. Every zone is written as an offset from it, such as UTC+1 or UTC−5.
-   **GMT** is the UK’s winter time and, for everyday use, the same as UTC. In summer the UK uses BST, UTC+1.
-   **Abbreviations** like EST, CET or AEST are handy but ambiguous (CST is used in the US, China and Cuba), so the tool also shows the UTC offset and the official zone name, such as America/Chicago.

## Frequently asked questions

How do I find the time difference between two places?

The first box starts with your device's time zone. Search a city or country in the second box, such as New York, London, Dubai, Karachi or Sydney. The tool shows the time in both places now, how many hours ahead or behind the second one is, and an hour-by-hour table for the next day.

Does it include daylight saving time?

Yes. Times follow the current rules for each place, and the tool tells you the date of the next clock change in either place, which can change the difference between them by an hour.

Why does the difference change during the year?

Because many countries move their clocks on different dates, and some never do. The UK and the EU change on the last Sundays of March and October, the US and Canada on the second Sunday of March and the first Sunday of November, and Australia's south-eastern states in early October and early April. In the weeks between the American and European changes, London and New York are 4 hours apart instead of 5.

Why are some time zones 30 or 45 minutes off?

Some countries chose an offset in between whole hours: India is UTC+5:30, Iran UTC+3:30, Newfoundland UTC−3:30, central Australia UTC+9:30 and Nepal UTC+5:45. The tool handles these the same as any other zone.

What is the best time to call someone abroad?

Look for the green rows in the table: hours that fall between 9:00 and 17:00 in both places. When there are none, the yellow rows show hours when both people are likely to be awake.
